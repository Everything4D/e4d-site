Rescue · Developer deceased, no source

Recovering a business's data from a time-bombed system

A small business owner's 4D system, written in the late 1990s, stopped starting: a hard-coded license expiration she never knew about, and a developer who had passed away and left no source code or successor. We reverse-engineered the datafile and recovered the great majority of her data. It's the case that explains why E4D exists.

Platform
Legacy 4D, binary
Outcome
Data recovered; system rebuilt as a new project

What they have in common

Long-lived systems, and the people responsible for them.

Our clients are rarely technology companies. They are businesses that, at some point, had a system built for them — often by one person — and discovered it worked so well that it became the way the company runs. Years later, someone has to keep it running, make it talk to the modern world, and make sure it survives the next transition.

That someone is usually not a developer. They are an owner, an operations manager, or the person who got handed the keys. We work well with them, because two of us have been them.

Sound familiar?
